# ADR 2026-10.3y2s — The Knit watch face is a WFF v4 face fed by a ring complication

Status: Accepted (2026-10-13)

**What was asked, and what stood in the way.** A bespoke Knit watch face: the time, three complication slots, and a
bezel ring of short dashes (a "running stitch") where each of the phone's live links lights a run in its plane's
colour — Bluetooth, Wi-Fi Aware, LoRa, internet relay — a link on two planes striping, unused runs grey. ADR
2026-09.wetm passed on a custom face because a face that can ship runs no code; this is the face it left for later.
The phone already sends what the ring needs: `WearExtra.links`, one plane mask per link, short-range first.

**What changed.** A new resource-only module, `:watchface`, holds a Watch Face Format **v4** face (Wear OS 6, API
36), built beside `:wear` under the same `-Pknit.wear=true` switch. A new source in `:wear`,
`RingComplicationService`, answers `LONG_TEXT` with `RingCode`: 97 characters, the reading's state (`L` live, `A`
aged, `R` resting — off, paused or no radio — `X` out of reach) and then one plane code per dash (`0` unlit,
`1`–`4` the planes) for two rounds of 48. Links are knitted in rounds of three: the outer round is a full turn at
three links, a third each; a fourth starts a thinner inner round, which the face shows only once its first run's
first dash (dash 1) is lit; a seventh and eighth (the phone sends at most eight, far peers last) share the inner
round. Dash 0 of each round is the cast-on point under the node and never lit; the other 47 split into runs as even
as whole dashes allow (15, 16, 16 for three links). Each run's last dash stays unlit so runs read apart. Which link
lands on which dash is decided in Kotlin; the face only prints the code. The face binds that source to a fixed,
non-customizable slot over the whole dial (`DefaultProviderPolicy` `primaryProvider`, bounded by a full-dial
`BoundingOval`; the user slots come later in the scene, so their taps win), and every dash reads its own character
with `subText` and picks its colour with a v4 colour `Transform`; an aged reading draws at about a third. An unlit
"fabric" ring is drawn under the slot outside it, so a face without Knit installed, or before the grant, still
looks whole. The three user slots default to Knit's carrying source (top) and the system's heart rate and steps,
and take short text, ranged, goal, small and monochrome images.

**It is dressed in the launcher icon's materials**, because a first version with neutral grey fabric read as any
face with a gauge, not Knit. The icon is a K drawn in rounded coral dashes on maroon with a diamond node where the
strokes meet, which is this ring's vocabulary already. So the unlit fabric and the slot fills are the icon's maroon
(`#3A1B16`, a shade darker under the slots); the icon's diamond node, in its coral (`#E35D4B`), sits at 12 o'clock
over both rounds' cast-on dash, where the knitting starts; the time's colon is two coral dots, the hours and
minutes being separate `TimeText`s either side of them; and a coral needle, a dot just outside the ring, steps
round with the seconds. Always-on keeps the node (dimmed) and turns the colon grey; the needle hides. The planes
are the brand's colours too: the short-range ones are the icon's two strokes, Bluetooth its coral (`#E35D4B`) and
Wi-Fi Aware its highlight (`#F2918D`), so the everyday ring is Knit's own yarn; the long-range ones are the coral's
analogous neighbours, LoRa amber (`#F0A03C`) and the internet relay raspberry (`#D2508A`), so a far peer stands out
without leaving the family. An all-coral ring (four shades told apart by lightness alone) was tried first and lost
the planes: the relay's brick passed for an unlit dash.

The alternatives a reader reaches for first do not fit. `WEIGHTED_ELEMENTS` carries per-element colours a v4 face
can read, but at most seven elements, and eight runs with their gaps and stripes need up to sixteen. A
`PHOTO_IMAGE` drawn by the app is discouraged in always-on and resends a bitmap on every reading. Eight separate
link slots plus three user slots pass the eight-slot cap. A legacy `androidx.wear.watchface` face in Kotlin could
read the snapshot directly, but Play has refused to install legacy faces since 14 January 2026, and watches
launched on Wear OS 5 or 6 do not run them. v4 rather than v2 because a v2 face can only recolour by stacking a
pre-coloured copy of every dash per plane and toggling their alpha.

`watchface/src/main/res/raw/watchface.xml` is generated by `scripts/gen-watchface.py` (geometry and palette live
there), never edited: ninety-six colour expressions are a typo nobody sees. `scripts/check-watchface.sh` checks the
file is fresh, the APK carries no dex (both build types are minified — without R8 the debug APK packages AGP's own
classes), the XML is valid v4 by Google's validator and the APK fits Google's memory budget, with both tools built
from `google/watchface` at one pinned commit into the git dir.

**What it costs, and the traps.** The ring's code is the face's only contract with `:wear`: `RingCodeTest` pins it
on the Kotlin side and the generator's docstring names it; change the two together. The source shows a code, not
words, in any other face — its picker label says it is for the Knit face. The face's package,
`app.getknit.knit.watchfacepush.stitch`, is Watch Face Push's naming, chosen now so a later push from `:wear` needs
no new package; a face may not share `:wear`'s id. Both Knit sources the face binds list that package as
`SAFE_WATCH_FACES` so the face needs no complication grant — documented for faces with code, and confirmed for this
WFF face on the first trial: no prompt when the face was picked. The ring slot must not be bounded by a
`BoundingArc`: the runtime fits a slot's content to its bounding shape, and an arc over the bezel squashed all 48
dashes into one dot at 12 o'clock — valid XML, a grey ring on the watch. String equality in WFF expressions
(`subText(...) == "1"`) is undocumented but works; the validator checks syntax, not meaning, so a probe face on the
watch is the only test of an expression. The ring is as fresh as the readings ADR 2026-09.wetm already bounds: five
minutes on the alarm while a Knit complication is active, aged past six.

Rounds of three replaced a first layout of six dashes per link slot, which left the dial half empty at four links —
a crowd that rarely happens — and looked broken at one or two.

First device trial, 2026-10-13, Pixel Watch 3 (API 37) paired to a debug phone meshing with four nodes: after the
bounds fix the ring drew the code the runtime logged (`DWF:WearComplicationProvider [n:TEXT]`), Bluetooth runs and
a Bluetooth + Wi-Fi Aware run striped, and both Knit slots bound and filled.

This is a prototype, sideload only, like `:wear`; CI builds neither. Still owed before anyone else wears it, on a
Wear OS 6 watch paired to a debug phone: the ring's runs match the app's peer map and Diagnostics; the mesh stopped
dims the ring within six minutes and a failed read blanks it; a pause greys it; always-on keeps only thin grey
dashes, the time, the date and the slots' contents; a ranged source in a bottom slot draws its arc. Distribution —
a Play listing of its own, or Watch Face Push from `:wear` for F-Droid, whose Play-services dependency is
unconfirmed — is a later amendment.

## Amendment 2026-10-13 — the monogram, an analog sibling

A second face, `watchface-monogram/` (`app.getknit.knit.watchfacepush.monogram`, "Knit Monogram"), is a branded
analog face, for fun and for the store page. A WFF APK holds one face, so it is its own module, behind the same
`-Pknit.wear=true` switch; `scripts/gen-watchface.py` writes both faces (they share the round user slots) and
`scripts/check-watchface.sh` checks both.

It is the launcher icon's "K" on an oxblood dial: the icon's maroon (the stitch face's fabric) in a radial glow,
falling to near-black at the bezel. The icon's flat slate was the first dial and read as a placeholder on the
wrist (device trial, 2026-10-13); the glow is one native `RadialGradient`, so the face stays vector. The spine's four dashes and the node are printed on the dial;
the hands are the K's two arms and pivot on the node's right corner, where the icon's arms meet, so at 1:22 (and
13:22) the face draws the icon. The geometry is the icon path's own cap centres, scaled by 0.6. Four choices came
out of mockups:

- **The node is one solid diamond**, not the icon's ring with four dots. With the ring, a hand pointing anywhere
  but right piled onto the node's corners and dots and the face read as a tangle at every time but 1:22.
- **The printed K is muted** (the stroke mixed about halfway into the dial) and only the hands are bright, so the hands never
  read as part of the static K. **The hour hand is the icon's deep coral and the minute hand its light coral**, and
  the minute hand alone carries a thin thread on to the track: both arms are the icon's length, so length cannot
  tell them apart.
- **For the minute of 1:22 the K lights up**: the printed K crossfades to the icon's colours, the hour hand turns
  light coral, and both hands snap to the icon's exact arm angles (43.4° and 135.4°; the true hands are within 3°
  of them all that minute). The rest of the day the K sits back. `LIT` in the generator is the one condition.
- **Two user slots at about 4 and 8 o'clock**, in off the track, clear of the spine, and far enough round that the 1:22 arm
  passes the right one by a hand's width. The left defaults to Knit's Nearby source, whose manifest lists the
  monogram as a safe face; the right to the system step count.

No link ring: the monogram reads no Knit data beyond the slot a user may change. Always-on is black with a dark
grey K, grey hands and the hour stitches. Owed on a watch: the 1:22 swap and snap happen on the minute and undo at
1:23; the hands' angle transforms track the time in always-on; the Nearby slot binds without a prompt.
